# Briarwell playtest: Morgan

Played as Wren, start to "Begin a New Adventure". 6 free-typed actions, 2 "talk" openers, 5 picked choices. Path: whispered with Ros and teased Mother, found Bess's doll, out-raced Dunstan, returned the doll, bought the ribbon, then the raid and Holloway. I never reached the Miller.

## 1. Verdict

**6/10.** When I was typing, the narrator played like a good DM: it said "yes, and" to everything, gave the NPCs real voices and picked up details I'd planted earlier. Then the scripted beats took over. The story skipped my third deed, left out the gift Ros asked for, and gave me a single action for the whole raid. I'd play the next chapter. I wouldn't bring my group yet, because they'd hit the same rails and notice.

## 2. Best moments

- **Ros's secret.** I knelt and whispered to her, and she named something specific with a seven-year-old's run-on logic: *"The ribbon doll. The one on Old Garrick's stall. With the yellow hair... I didn't ask because I knew we hadn't the coin."* Then the button: *"You promised not to tell."* That's a real character.
- **Mother overhearing.** *"Elin does not turn around. A long moment passes. Then her shoulders shift in what is unmistakably a suppressed laugh."* / *"Two copper coins, I believe. Or so I am told by a little bird..."* A dry, warm voice, and it handed me the stakes in character. A human DM would be proud of that.
- **Callbacks.** When I swore my oath to Bess, the narrator added *"the same spot you swore to Ros not an hour ago."* It remembered my bit and built on it.
- **Bess's doll had real detail.** *"One button eye and one eye that's just a knot of thread because the button fell off last winter."* When I found her it echoed the same details back, and Bess's lead ("Dunstan came hollering... I must have dropped her there") tied two quests together in a natural way.
- **The Dunstan race.** I picked up the bullied kid's cap, then challenged Dunstan to a race with flour sacks as the stake, and the DM ran with all of it. *"A boy like Dunstan cannot refuse a race in front of an audience, and you both know it."* He spat on his palm to shake on it. I also loved *"you never said anything about dropping her"* (Wren kept hold of the doll). Dunstan's sulky *go on, then* gesture was earned.
- **The raid, written as prose.** I barricaded the door with the table, and Father's look was *"pride and terror in the same breath."* It hit hard.

## 3. Worst moments / frustrations

- **The story pulled me out of the village.** I handed Bess her doll and was teleported straight to Garrick's stall, then teleported home. A toast said "Left unfinished: The Miller's Secret". I'd been told *"any two good turns"*, but nothing warned me that the second coin ends the day. I wanted to see Hob, and I wanted a beat with Dunstan, who had followed me to the square. A good DM would say: "You've got your two coins. The sun's getting low. Head to Garrick's, or poke around a bit more?"
- **Garrick didn't have the ribbon doll.** The best setup in the game was Ros wanting *the ribbon doll with yellow hair* at *two coppers*. Garrick offered sweets, a cornflower ribbon and a wooden bird, and the box was choice-only, so I couldn't ask for the doll. He also said *"your coin buys any one of these"* while I had two coins. Then Ros's reaction was canned: *"It's perfect! You're the best!"* She didn't mention the ribbon or her wish at all. A human DM would have paid that off ("She's got the doll... no, wait, a *ribbon*? For the doll's hair!") or given me a hook ("Garrick sold the doll this morning...").
- **The raid gets one action.** I typed one action (poker, ribbon, table) and the narrator resolved the whole night in that single turn, through to me being knocked down. The objective said *"hide, fight, or search for Mother and Ros"*, but I never got to choose a second time. A DM would stop at "the door gives, two men are through, Father steps in front of you. What do you do?"
- **The race resolved itself.** There was no roll and no "do you cut the corner by the mill?" I just won. That's fine for a kid's prologue, but some tension would have made it land.
- **Breaking the fourth wall.** *"the story quietly presses it into your hand: a deed done is a deed paid."* That reads like the engine explaining its reward rule. Have Dunstan toss the coin, or the boy whose cap I saved press one on me.
- **The "ask Father" choice changes nothing.** "Ask Father what's wrong" got *"Nothing for you to worry about. Bed. Now."*, which is the same result as going to bed.
- **The narration and the map disagree:**
  - The narrator says Dunstan *"has cornered two small children against the hedgerow"*, but no children are on the map and Dunstan stands next to me.
  - The intro mentions *"a smaller child's sobbing"*, and the square promises *"gossiping neighbours"*. Neither is on screen.
  - Mother *"stands at the hearth turning the birthday loaf"* while her sprite is outdoors beside the cottage.
  - During the raid, Mother and Ros *"are gone into the smoke"*, but their sprites are still sitting in the cottage.

## 4. Bugs and glitches

1. **Stale objective toast during the climax.** After I typed my raid action, a green "New objective: Choose a gift for Ros at Old Garrick's stall." appeared. It stayed through the Holloway scene and is visible in the screenshot at the stranger choice. Repro: buy the gift, go to bed, type any action in "Briarwell Burns".
2. **"Left unfinished: The Miller's Secret" stays on screen.** It sat on screen from the Bess hand-in through Garrick's stall, the birthday and the raid.
3. **Floating name labels with no sprite.**
   - A "Dunstan" label hung in mid-air in the Meadow after he walked off. Repro: win the race, then press Escape and look.
   - A "Little Bess" label sat on the well in the next screen, both in the dark cottage interior during the raid and in the outdoor Holloway scene.
4. **The burned cottage looks untouched.** The description says *"Blackened beams and grey ash where the cottage stood"*, but the cottage sprite is intact (only dimmed).
5. **The player sprite wanders off its mark.** After I searched the crooked fence post, Wren ended up well east of the post, next to the bushes. During the Ros whisper, the SCENE line showed the player at two different positions.
6. **The fence traps you at the cottage start.** Walking right does nothing because Father, then the garden fence, block the way. I had to go down two tiles first. A first-time player may think the controls are broken.
7. **Garrick mentions one coin when I had two.**
8. **The quest text points to the wrong place.** The objective says Bess's doll is in the Village Square, but it's in the Meadow. That's a fine twist, but the quest text should be vaguer.
9. **Typewriter text gets cut off.** If you press Enter while the typewriter is running, the line can jump ahead before you've read it. For example, *"Two coins clink in your pocket as y"* was gone before I finished it.

## 5. Pacing and responsiveness

- **AI turns took 14–38s.** The 30s+ turns (the race and the raid) felt long. A "the DM is thinking" beat or streaming the first line sooner would help.
- **Reading pace was good.** Paragraphs are a nice length, and the "more to read" cue is clear.
- **Walking between areas is quick and pleasant.** The maps are small enough not to drag.
- **The back half rushes.** The first half breathes. From the doll hand-in to the end, it's about six screens with no free input except the raid. I spent more time talking to Ros about her birthday than I spent in the raid.
- **The whole prologue took about 20 minutes and felt short.** The fun was in the free-typed scenes, and there weren't enough of them.

## 6. What would make me play more (most important first)

1. **Let me finish the day.** Don't end it the moment the second coin lands. Offer to move on, and keep the third deed (the Miller) open until I head to Garrick's.
2. **Pay off the setups the players reveal.** If Ros wants the ribbon doll, Garrick should have it, or the story should explain why not. Ros's reaction to the gift should come from the AI and name the actual gift.
3. **Break the climax into beats that each ask for my input.** At least 2–3 player turns in "Briarwell Burns": the door, finding Mother, the fall. Make "hide / fight / search" real forks with different outcomes.
4. **Allow free text at the stall and with Holloway**, or add a "say something else" option. Choice-only buttons after an hour of free roleplay feel like the DM took the dice away.
5. **Put the people the narrator mentions on the map**: the bullied children, the gossips, bandits at the door. When the map and the text disagree, the illusion breaks.
6. **Add some uncertainty to contests** (races, fights), even a visible roll, so that winning feels earned.
7. **Clean up the HUD**: stale toasts, ghost labels, and the cottage sprite after the fire.
8. **Keep the narrator in the fiction.** No "the story presses a coin into your hand."
